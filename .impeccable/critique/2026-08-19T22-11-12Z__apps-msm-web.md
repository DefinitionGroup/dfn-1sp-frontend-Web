## Design Specificity Verdict

**The whole app fails the specificity test, but contains excellent authored islands.**

The Units overview and XR Labs page feel unmistakably MSM.digital. Their full-bleed media, hard divisions, disciplined cyan signals, square geometry, Aspekta typography, and living cube mark deliver the intended “Vast Space” world.

The rest of the app often feels inherited rather than authored. Cases changes the mobile identity to 1SP, shared case copy promotes 1SP, Contact becomes a generic light/violet form, and Services does not carry a strong MSM signal. Remove the navigation and several pages could belong to almost any technology agency.

The deterministic scan supports that diagnosis: **66 findings across 29 files**, comprising **14 warnings and 52 advisories**. The strongest clusters are **28 ungoverned font sizes** and **21 off-system colors**, followed by gradient text, side-tab patterns, gray-on-color contrast, and radius drift. Representative locations include `pg-ResultsMetrics.tsx`, `pg-PageBuilderPersonioJobs.tsx`, `ScrollHighlight.tsx`, `TextHeadlineCombo.tsx`, `glass-surface.module.css`, and `percentagePosNegDiagram.tsx`.

Some findings are legitimate exceptions rather than defects: the hard-coded colors in `MsmLogoAnimated.tsx` encode the brand mark, and high radii inside scrollbar/globe geometry are not rounded content cards. The scanner’s generic “AI palette” label is also misleading—the real issue is uncontrolled violet drift, not AI authorship.

No user-visible detector overlay is available. Mutable script injection failed because the browser evaluation surface exposed `document.title` as read-only. Evidence therefore came from isolated desktop/mobile screenshots, DOM/layout metrics, console logs, and HTTP checks.

## Overall Impression

The app has the ingredients of a remarkable MSM site, but only the new Unit surfaces consistently assemble them. Right now it reads as a strong new identity layered over a mixed archive of 1SP and experimental page-builder components.

The biggest opportunity is not adding more visual invention. It is establishing one MSM presentation contract and forcing every shared surface through it.

## What’s Working

1. **Units is the clearest expression of “Vast Space.”** It is expansive without becoming decorative, angular without becoming brutal, and branded without spraying cube colors everywhere.
2. **XR Labs connects atmosphere to proposition exceptionally well.** The spatial image, descriptor, claim, animated mark, and capability list form one coherent argument.
3. **Case storytelling has strong editorial pacing.** The inspected case moves well from cinematic hero through narrative, proof, future outlook, and contact. The problem is channel framing, not the underlying story structure.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] The conversion architecture is unreliable

**What:** The homepage “Start a project” CTA points to `/en/legal`; “Call us” opens a contact form; several service actions use `href="#"`; `/contact` and `/en/contact` coexist.

**Why it matters:** The highest-intent visitor can be sent to legal content, an ambiguous route, or nowhere. Visual polish cannot compensate for broken intent.

**Fix:** Establish one locale-aware contact-link utility and one CTA contract. Audit every hero, service, Unit, case, and footer destination. Use buttons for in-page controls and links only for actual destinations.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`

### [P1] Navigation and brand identity fracture by route

**What:** Mobile Cases displays a prominent **1SP Agency** identity, while most other inspected mobile first viewports show no global navigation affordance. Desktop navigation also introduces a rounded glass language against MSM’s square geometry.

**Why it matters:** Visitors can feel trapped or believe they have moved to another company. The most reusable brand surface is not channel-safe.

**Fix:** Create one MSM navigation contract for desktop and mobile: MSM mark, canonical route set, predictable visibility, square geometry, consistent active state, and no 1SP asset leakage.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt`

### [P1] Shared and legacy surfaces escape the MSM visual system

**What:** Case copy promotes 1SP; broad violet/orange gradients violate the scarce-signal rule; Contact uses violet focus styling and a generic white form; Services lacks a meaningful MSM brand signal. The detector found widespread local colors, sizes, gradients, and even an Arial fallback.

**Why it matters:** The app reads as stitched products rather than one authored experience. Shared content is architecturally sensible, but public presentation still needs an MSM-owned frame.

**Fix:** Build an MSM presentation adapter for shared Case, Service, CTA, form, metric, and navigation components. Centralize type scales and channel-safe color/geometry variants instead of correcting isolated classes.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable polish`

### [P1] Contact publicly exposes an unfinished editorial state

**What:** The page displays: “Get in touch with MSM. The contact form renders automatically below the page-builder content on this page.”

**Why it matters:** This is the trust-critical conversion moment, and it reveals implementation mechanics. The generic form styling reinforces the impression that the page is unfinished.

**Fix:** Remove the implementation note. Recompose the page as an MSM surface, add a response-time expectation and privacy destination, and route visitors by Unit or named contact where useful.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### [P2] Mobile discovery is overloaded and less accessible

**What:** Cases presents 12 filters at once, with roughly 30px-high targets, followed by a compressed two-column mobile gallery. Services includes icon-only controls without accessible names.

**Why it matters:** The taxonomy becomes the interface, touch accuracy suffers, titles become cramped, and assistive-technology users cannot understand the service controls.

**Fix:** Put filters behind one disclosure or make the four Units the primary filter model; use at least 44px targets; use one confident mobile case column; label all carousel controls.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill`

## Cognitive Load

The app fails **5 of 8** checks, which is high cognitive load. Single focus fails on the Services prelude and Contact implementation note. Chunking and minimal choices fail at the 12-option Cases filter decision. Visual hierarchy fails across routes because the design language changes. Working memory fails because visitors must infer Unit ownership and expertise. Progressive disclosure fails in Cases and opaque Services controls. Grouping and linear editorial storytelling are comparatively strong.

The most overloaded moment is the mobile Cases index: twelve small filters immediately followed by a dense two-column gallery.

## Emotional Journey

The opening is energetic and technically credible. Units is the emotional peak: premium, focused, and recognizably MSM.

Confidence drops on inherited surfaces. Services opens with a visually dead prelude; Cases switches identity to 1SP; shared case copy and gradients violate the MSM promise. Contact is the emotional low point because internal publishing instructions appear at the moment the visitor is deciding whether to trust the company.

The footer restores some confidence, but the conversion endpoint still lacks reassurance: no response expectation, clear privacy cue, named contact, or Unit routing.

## Minor Observations

- Homepage copy uses “Europes” instead of “Europe’s.”
- Homepage and Contact have no `h1`; Services places lower-level headings before its `h1`.
- The rotating homepage headline duplicates phrases in the accessibility tree.
- “Call us” promises a phone interaction but routes to a form.
- Footer information architecture is useful but unusually dense on mobile.
- No horizontal overflow was found at 390px, and all inspected routes returned 200.
- Console output was clean apart from the expected localhost Cookiebot warning.

## Questions to Consider

- Is MSM.digital genuinely standalone, or a themed doorway into 1SP? The current mobile experience argues for the latter.
- Should a Unit page launch without at least one leader and one attributed case?
- Is the primary conversion promise “talk to MSM,” “contact a Unit leader,” or “enter the wider 1SP network”?
- Would the four Units make a better primary discovery model than twelve service tags?
